# India local content, sources and recipes

Reviewed 6 November 2026. Sixty registered components; twenty-six curated guides; 146 explicit area recipes including inventoried legacy labels. New guides remain unpublished until administrator review.

## Component inventory

The shared introduction, working search and results remain first. Search and results are two marked parts of the same discovery state machine. Reserved components have different semantic structures and, where appropriate, different local interactions; the contact sheets show their actual rendered designs.

- **introduction**: existing discovery functionality with India-local presentation.
- **search**: existing discovery functionality with India-local presentation.
- **results**: existing discovery functionality with India-local presentation.
- **state-city** — Start with the city where care is being provided. A state overview can help you choose a starting point. The collecting hospital’s city is usually more useful for a contact search than a broad state name.
- **district-city** — A familiar place name can mean more than one area. Cities, districts and states can share names. A matching label does not prove that a person is close to the hospital or able to travel.
- **location-index** — Choose a location you can act on. Use the reviewed city links as a starting point. Each contact keeps its own recorded location, including when results extend beyond your first choice.
- **coverage-ledger** — Know what the local figures actually count. A useful directory separates account participation from manually maintained contacts and organization listings. None of these figures is a blood-stock total.
- **scope-diagram** — Local coverage has boundaries. A city label, a district and a metropolitan region are not interchangeable. This guide explains its geographic scope rather than promising coverage across a whole region.
- **location-guide** — Find the right place before the right contact. Start with the collecting hospital, then choose a city and blood group. If your location is not listed, check official resources and read broader suggestions carefully.
- **filter-tour** — A focused search is easier to use. Use the blood group requested by the care team and the collecting city. Search filters organize directory records; they do not establish a clinical match.
- **source-lanes** — Three record types. Three different meanings. People and organizations can both be useful starting points. Their cards should not be interpreted as the same kind of participation.
- **broader-flow** — Local results first, with context when the search widens. The existing search starts with the page’s location and selected category. Broader suggestions keep their actual location so you can judge the next step.
- **card-anatomy** — Read a contact card before you call. A name and blood group do not tell the full story. The source label, city and permitted contact action help you understand what a result represents.
- **count-explainer** — One volunteer can appear in more than one city. Service-city participation helps people offer support in places they can reasonably serve. Adding city totals can therefore count the same person more than once.
- **limited-coverage** — A small directory can become more useful through accurate participation. Where local entries are limited, there may be fewer useful starting points. Registering your own details can help build the picture without promising a response or medical outcome.
- **request-worksheet** — Prepare a clear request before reaching out. Keep the collecting hospital, requested component, timing and coordinator together. Avoid putting patient documents or unnecessary private information into public messages.
- **coordinator-checklist** — Give one person a clear coordination role. A shared plan reduces repeated calls and conflicting instructions. Agree who will confirm the request and tell contacts when arrangements change.
- **hospital-questions** — Ask the hospital what happens next. A directory cannot replace collection instructions. Get the practical details from the treating or collecting service before asking anyone to attend.
- **timing-handoff** — Keep timing clear at each handoff. A contact may be willing to help but unable to attend at the requested time. Agree the timing directly and keep everyone informed when it changes.
- **before-travel** — A short confirmation can prevent an unnecessary journey. Do not ask someone to travel on the strength of an old message or an unconfirmed listing. Check the requirement again and agree the destination.
- **request-updates** — The request needs an owner after the first message. Responsibility does not end when a link is shared. Keep the people you contacted informed and avoid leaving outdated requests in circulation.
- **signup-journey** — Register with details you can maintain. A useful profile starts with accurate information. Follow the current verification steps and review your participation choices after signing in.
- **availability-demo** — Willingness can change. Your profile should reflect that. Availability is a participation setting, not a medical assessment. Keep it current and confirm each request through the appropriate collecting service.
- **service-planner** — Choose service cities thoughtfully. Add places where you can reasonably participate. A service-city entry does not promise transport, immediate attendance or availability for every request.
- **contact-preferences** — Make contact choices deliberately. Public discovery and private account records have different purposes. Review the controls available to you instead of sharing more information than coordination needs.
- **profile-maintenance** — An accurate profile stays useful after signup. Return to your existing account when details change. A second registration is less useful than keeping one profile current.
- **first-profile** — Your first contribution can be a clear, accurate profile. You do not need a public story or a large network to participate. Register your own details, understand the controls and decide how you can help.
- **fictional-timeline** — A private record of care, kept in order. This demonstration uses fictional information. Your own reports and prescriptions belong in protected account tools, separate from the public donor directory.
- **report-folder** — Make the right report easier to find. Recognizable titles and dates help you organize documents. Storage does not interpret a report or replace advice from a qualified professional.
- **visit-checklist** — Bring a clear record to your next appointment. A private profile can help you organize documents before a visit. Ask your clinician which records are relevant rather than assuming every file is needed.
- **prescription-roadmap** — Know which prescription features are working for you. Uploaded copies and participating-platform delivery are different workflows. An integration must be configured and authorized before a clinician can submit to your profile.
- **public-private** — Public participation and private care records stay separate. Helping people find a contact does not require exposing medical files. Keep each kind of information in the part of the platform designed for it.
- **record-walkthrough** — Save a document with a future visit in mind. Use the existing account workflow to upload, organize and find your files. This page demonstrates the steps without uploading anything.
- **official-directory** — Use official resources for the next clinical step. BloodNet’s contacts and external blood-centre information answer different questions. Review official guidance and confirm current requirements with the collecting service.
- **contacts-stock** — A donor contact is not a blood unit. A directory entry records a person’s participation or a listed contact. Stock availability is maintained by collecting services and must be checked separately.
- **collecting-roles** — Keep clinical decisions with the right people. Clear roles help everyone use the directory safely. BloodNet supports discovery; collecting and treating services retain their clinical responsibilities.
- **component-glossary** — Confirm the component, not just the blood group. Whole blood, red cells, platelets and plasma are different terms. This glossary helps you ask a clearer question; it does not recommend a product or a substitute.
- **source-provenance** — Understand where each kind of information comes from. The platform’s records support its participation figures. External health resources explain their own services and guidance. Neither is a guarantee of an individual response.
- **screening-path** — Registration comes before a separate eligibility decision. A BloodNet profile does not certify that someone can donate. The collecting service assesses eligibility and gives the instructions relevant to that donation.
- **resident-invitation** — Local participation starts with people who choose to join. An accurate location and a current contact can give your community another starting point. Join voluntarily and keep your expectations realistic.
- **family-roles** — Help a family coordinate without oversharing. One person can confirm hospital instructions while another manages agreed contacts. Keep medical documents private and use one consistent update.
- **student-awareness** — Share a useful explanation, not pressure to participate. Students can help others understand a donor directory and official donation guidance. No affiliation with a school or college is implied.
- **workplace-support** — Make workplace participation voluntary. A colleague can share the service or help coordinate an agreed request. Participation should not become an obligation or a public medical disclosure.
- **organizer-consent** — Ask before publishing someone’s story or contact. Community updates need clear permission and accurate context. A patient’s documents are not promotional material, even when a request is urgent.
- **responsible-sharing** — A clear message can travel without exposing a patient. Share a directory link and the practical coordination details approved by the care team. Do not circulate prescriptions, identity documents or unverified claims.
- **same-state** — Explore another reviewed city in the same state. Related city guides can help you understand the directory’s geography. A nearby or same-state link does not confirm travel distance or available donors.
- **state-navigator** — Step back to the state, then choose a city. State guides connect reviewed city mappings without claiming complete statewide coverage. Use them to orient yourself before opening a local directory.
- **group-links** — Continue with a blood group supported by recorded entries. Group links appear only where the directory has relevant individual entries. Organization-only coverage does not create blood-group availability.
- **published-reading** — Read something that helps with the next step. Practical guides can make coordination clearer. They supplement the collecting service’s instructions rather than replacing them.
- **organization-explorer** — Read organization contacts on their own terms. An organization listing is a contact record. It does not establish partnership, accreditation, stock, opening hours or a guaranteed response.
- **next-step** — Choose the next step that fits your situation. You may need a contact, an explanation or your own account. Choose one useful action rather than treating every page as a promise of immediate help.
- **local-faq** — Clear answers before you use the directory. Understand the local scope, source labels and clinical limits before contacting someone. Ask the collecting service about medical requirements.
- **definition-disclosures** — Keep the definitions beside the numbers. A source label changes how a figure should be read. The same is true for state, city and worldwide totals.
- **scope-facts** — Useful facts, without bigger promises. A directory can make a starting point easier to find. It cannot make clinical or geographic promises that its records do not support.
- **service-status** — Know what is available and what needs configuration. Private account tools and provider integrations are different capabilities. A planned service must not look like an active booking destination.
- **response-expectations** — A message is an invitation to respond. People and organizations may not be able to reply or participate. Do not wait for an online response when medical care is urgent.
- **closing-invitation** — One accurate profile can add another starting point. Register if you want to participate, or maintain your existing account if you already have one. Clear details are more useful than repeated registrations.
- **state-evidence** — See the evidence behind this state overview. The state total uses reviewed city mappings, with accounts de-duplicated across service cities. It is not a measure of blood inventory or complete state coverage.
- **regional-update** — Local updates need real context and permission. Approved regional updates can explain community activity. When no update is published, this guide explains how to contribute responsibly rather than inventing a story.
- **organ-pathway** — An organ pledge expresses a wish, not an allocation. Use authorized medical services for organ-donation decisions and transplantation. A BloodNet listing is not official donor registration, an allocation system or a confirmed clinical match.
